README --- NEXT-GENERATION BATTERY THERMAL MANAGEMENT BEYOND COOLING
1. MANUSCRIPT INFORMATION
TITLE: Next-Generation Battery Thermal Management Beyond Cooling:
Multiscale Heat Transport, Adaptive Materials and Intelligent Heat
Dissipation---A 2055 Research Perspective

ARTICLE TYPE: Critical review / research-perspective review

REVIEW DESIGN: PRISMA 2020-guided literature screening

EVIDENCE BASE REPORTED IN THE ABSTRACT: 132 peer-reviewed journal
articles
2. PURPOSE AND SCOPE
This review examines the transition from conventional battery cooling to
multiscale battery thermal management. It considers thermal management
as an integrated function involving heat generation, heat transport,
thermal buffering, heat dissipation, sensing and intelligent control.

The manuscript covers:
- Heat generation and operating-temperature requirements in battery
  cells.
- Multiscale heat transport from the cell level to modules and packs.
- Passive thermal buffering using phase change materials (PCMs) and
  adaptive composites.
- Hybrid thermal management, including liquid, air, immersion,
  heat-pipe and thermoelectric approaches.
- Intelligent management using digital twins, machine-learning
  surrogates, model predictive control (MPC) and reinforcement
  learning.
- Internal temperature measurement, virtual temperature sensing and
  wireless battery management systems.
- Biomimetic and bio-inspired heat-transfer structures.
- Thermal behaviour and safety considerations for solid-state and
  next-generation batteries.
- A three-horizon research and technology-readiness roadmap extending
  to 2055.
3. CENTRAL MESSAGE
The review argues that battery thermal management should no longer be
assessed only by its ability to keep peak cell temperature below a
limit. Relevant design objectives also include cell-to-cell temperature
uniformity, heating and cooling capability, response under changing
loads, energy demand, safety and system-level readiness.

A central principle of the review is that reported performance must be
interpreted in the context of its validation setting. Simulation-only
results, cell experiments, module tests, pack measurements and
hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) demonstrations are not equivalent forms of
evidence.
4. HIGHLIGHTS
- Heat buffering, spreading and control define battery thermal
  management in this review.
- PRISMA 2020 screening retained 132 journal studies, with validation
  levels recorded.
- Energy savings from intelligent controllers depend on the baseline
  and test setting.
- Solid-state battery thermal-runaway onset depends on chemistry,
  specimen and criterion.
- Readiness levels for 2035--2055 are linked to explicit advancement
  criteria.
